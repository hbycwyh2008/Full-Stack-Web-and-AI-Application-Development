# Lesson 00 — What Is Computer Science? + AP CSP Discovery

**Prerequisite:** [Required Orientation — Learn the Course Through the Four Posters](required-orientation-four-posters.md)  
**Length:** 90 minutes  
**Mission:** Build and revise a broader model of computer science, distinguish computer science from programming, and discover how this course overlaps with AP CSP without turning the lesson into an AP CSP lecture.  
**Required Input Resource:** [Khan Academy — What is computer science?](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/in-class-11-computer-science/xab724c3e878917b8%3Acomputer-system/xab724c3e878917b8%3Asoftware/v/what-is-computer-science)  
**Required Activity Resources:** [AP CSP CED Discovery Handout](ap-csp-ced-discovery-handout.md) + teacher-provided CS field/evidence cards  
**Durable Evidence:** video note-catcher + revised CS model + CS field map + AP CSP preparation/gap map + individual exit reflection

The four course posters come first. This lesson assumes students already understand the classroom flow, mastery expectations, AI-use expectations, and the optional role of competition pathways.

This lesson intentionally follows the learning sequence:

> **Initial idea → independent input → evidence investigation → peer explanation → model revision → transfer**

The Khan Academy video is a **first model**, not a definition students are expected to copy or memorize. Students should test and expand that model using the evidence in the rest of the lesson.

---

## 0–10 min — Entry Point Check: What Counts as Computer Science?

**Work individually. Do not search for a definition yet.**

Sort these examples into **Definitely Computer Science / Maybe / Not Primarily Computer Science**:

- write a JavaScript function;
- design an accessible interface;
- repair a cracked laptop screen;
- analyze how fast two algorithms scale;
- train an image classifier;
- route packets when one route fails;
- create a spreadsheet formula;
- review a pull request for correctness.

Choose one item you are unsure about and write a short reason.

Then complete this sentence using only your current thinking:

> **Computer science is ________________________________________________.**

Keep this first definition. You will revise it later.

---

## 10–22 min — Independent Input: Khan Academy

Watch:

**Khan Academy — [What is computer science?](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/in-class-11-computer-science/xab724c3e878917b8%3Acomputer-system/xab724c3e878917b8%3Asoftware/v/what-is-computer-science)**

Watch independently. Pause or replay when needed.

### Video Note-Catcher

Record concise answers while watching:

1. According to the video, what is a **program**?
2. What does a **programmer** do?
3. How does the video distinguish **computer programming** from **computer science**?
4. What kinds of questions might computer scientists study?
5. What fields does the video place under the computer-science umbrella?
6. Write one claim from the video that **changed, confirmed, or challenged** your original definition.

### First Revision

Without discussing with anyone yet, revise your opening sentence:

> **After the video, I would define computer science as ____________________________.**

Then mark one part of your new definition with:

- **C** = I feel confident about this claim;
- **?** = I still need evidence.

Do **not** treat the video as the final definition. The next task tests whether its model is broad enough.

---

## 22–35 min — Evidence Investigation: Is CS Bigger Than Programming?

Use the teacher-provided **CS field/evidence cards**.

Individually inspect examples from:

- Algorithms & Theory
- Data & Information
- Computer Systems & Networks
- Cybersecurity
- Human-Computer Interaction
- Software Engineering
- Artificial Intelligence
- Programming Languages / Abstraction
- Computing & Society

Record:

1. two examples that clearly involve programming;
2. two examples that are clearly computer science but are not simply “write code”;
3. one example that connects to more than one field;
4. one example that **expands or complicates** the Khan Academy video;
5. one sentence beginning: **“Computer science seems broader than programming because …”**

### Evidence Check

Return to the **?** in your video definition.

- Did any field card provide the evidence you needed?
- Did any field card make you revise the claim instead?

Annotate your definition with at least one piece of evidence.

---

## 35–48 min — Talk Round 1: Build a Better Working Definition

In pairs, compare:

- your Entry Point classifications;
- your video note-catchers;
- your field-card evidence;
- your revised definitions.

Each student must explain one disputed example and cite evidence from either the video or a field card.

Together produce:

- **one working definition of computer science**;
- **one sentence explaining where programming fits**;
- **one way the field evidence makes the definition broader or more precise than your first idea**;
- **one disagreement or uncertainty** you still have.

Do not aim for a textbook-perfect definition.

---

## 48–60 min — Build and Revise a CS Field Map

Teams of 3–4 build a **CS Field Map** by placing example cards around the field labels. A card may connect to more than one field.

Required group conclusion:

> **Programming is part of computer science because ____________________, but computer science is larger because ____________________.**

Groups must also identify:

- one example that belongs naturally to **multiple CS fields**;
- one example that a beginner might incorrectly classify as “just coding”;
- one disputed placement.

### Teacher Diagnosis — Only After Student Evidence

Listen for misconceptions first. Then give only the clarification the class actually needs.

A useful working definition is:

> **Computer science studies computation and information: what can be computed, how information is represented and processed, how algorithms and computational systems work, how efficiently and reliably they work, and how computing systems interact with people and society.**

Clarify only as needed:

- programming = expressing computational procedures and building systems;
- software engineering = disciplined development of software systems;
- computer engineering = stronger emphasis on hardware/electrical systems;
- IT = operation/support of computing infrastructure.

Students immediately revise at least one card placement or one sentence after the clarification.

### Definition Version 3

Students now write a third version:

> **My current definition of computer science is ________________________________.**

Under it, identify what changed across:

**Before video → After video → After evidence/discussion**

---

## 60–78 min — Mission Task: AP CSP CED Discovery

Open [AP CSP CED Discovery Handout](ap-csp-ced-discovery-handout.md).

This is a **discovery activity**, not an AP CSP content lesson.

Teams receive course-experience cards such as:

- user interview;
- Figma prototype;
- GitHub PR;
- JavaScript algorithm;
- debugging;
- working with data;
- usability testing;
- AI bias discussion.

Teams place each experience under the strongest likely AP CSP connection or **No Strong Direct Match**.

Then record:

- **two AP CSP areas this course helps prepare us for**;
- **two AP CSP areas we will still need to learn elsewhere/in AP CSP**;
- **one course experience that connects to more than one AP CSP idea**.

Do not force one-to-one matches.

A short teacher reveal may confirm that this course strongly supports areas such as Creative Development, Algorithms and Programming, Computational Solution Design, Abstraction, Code Analysis, collaboration, and Responsible Computing, while AP CSP also includes substantial content such as binary representation, compression, networking/fault tolerance, and AP-specific assessment skills.

---

## 78–87 min — Individual Exit Check: Transfer the Model

Work individually.

Complete:

1. **Computer science is not just ____________________.**
2. **Computer science studies ____________________.**
3. **Programming matters in CS because ____________________.**
4. **One piece of evidence that changed my model today was ____________________.**
5. **One part of this course that prepares me for AP CSP is ____________________.**
6. **One AP CSP area I will still need to learn later is ____________________.**

Then answer:

> A student says, “This class is basically a web-coding class.” What evidence from today's video, field investigation, and AP CSP discovery would you use to disagree, qualify, or agree?

Your answer must use **at least two specific pieces of evidence**.

---

## 87–90 min — Submission

Submit:

1. Entry Point classification + original definition;
2. Khan Academy video note-catcher;
3. definition revisions;
4. CS Field Map;
5. AP CSP preparation/gap map;
6. individual Exit Check.

## Success Evidence

Students can:

- distinguish computer science from computer programming without treating them as unrelated;
- explain that programming is important but not the whole of computer science;
- identify multiple CS fields and connect examples to them;
- revise a conceptual model after receiving new evidence;
- explain several genuine overlaps between this course and AP CSP;
- identify AP CSP content that this course does not attempt to replace.

## Teacher Note

Do not lecture the definition before students have produced and tested their own model.

The Khan Academy video supplies the **shared conceptual input** that the previous version of this lesson lacked. The field-card investigation then prevents the lesson from becoming passive video consumption: students must test the video's framing against broader evidence from HCI, systems, cybersecurity, AI, theory, software engineering, and computing & society.

Do not grade students on memorizing a perfect definition of CS or CED terminology. Grade the quality of their evidence, explanation, and model revision. The important change is that their mental model broadens beyond **CS = coding** and that they discover AP CSP connections through activity evidence.
