# Lesson 00 — What Is Computer Science? + AP CSP Discovery

**Prerequisite:** [Required Orientation — Learn the Course Through the Four Posters](required-orientation-four-posters.md)  
**Length:** 45 minutes  
**Mission:** Build and revise a broader model of computer science, distinguish computer science from programming, and discover how this course genuinely overlaps with AP CSP.

## Ready-to-Teach Materials

### Student file

[Lesson 00 Student Workbook Template](lesson-00-student-workbook-template.md)

Every student copies the template into their own learning repository as:

```text
reflections/lesson-00-what-is-computer-science.md
```

Students complete **all work directly in that Markdown file**. No printed worksheet and no paper cards are required.

### Independent input

The workbook links directly to:

[Khan Academy — What is computer science?](https://www.khanacademy.org/computing/in-class-11-computer-science/xab724c3e878917b8%3Acomputer-system/xab724c3e878917b8%3Asoftware/v/what-is-computer-science)

### Teacher reference

[Lesson 00 Teacher Key](lesson-00-teacher-key.md)

The key contains:

- suggested interpretation of the opening classification;
- Evidence Bank primary mappings and acceptable cross-field mappings;
- teacher clarification of CS vs programming / software engineering / IT / computer engineering;
- suggested AP CSP mappings;
- expected AP CSP gaps;
- success evidence.

## What the Teacher Needs to Prepare

Before class:

1. make sure students can access the course repository;
2. make sure students can create or edit a `.md` file;
3. give students the workbook-template link;
4. make sure the Khan Academy video is accessible on the school network.

That is the complete material setup.

**Do not prepare separate CS field cards, AP CSP cards, worksheets, or printed handouts.** The field labels, evidence items, AP CSP reference, course-experience bank, response tables, and exit check are already inside the student Markdown template.

---

# Learning Design

The lesson follows:

> **Initial model → independent input → evidence investigation → peer explanation → model revision → transfer**

The Khan Academy video is a **shared first model**, not a definition students copy as the final answer.

Students then test that model against an Evidence Bank covering:

- Algorithms & Theory
- Data & Information
- Computer Systems & Networks
- Cybersecurity
- Human-Computer Interaction
- Software Engineering
- Artificial Intelligence
- Programming Languages & Abstraction
- Computing & Society

The lesson ends by asking students to compare the actual course experiences with AP CSP rather than being told that the two courses are equivalent.

# 0–10 min — Entry Point: Build Version 1

## Student action

Students open their personal copy of:

```text
reflections/lesson-00-what-is-computer-science.md
```

They complete **Section 1 — Entry Point** independently.

They classify eight examples as:

- Definitely CS
- Maybe
- Not Primarily CS

Then they write:

> **Definition — Version 1: Computer science is ...**

## Teacher role

Do not define computer science yet.

Do not correct classifications while students are working.

The purpose is to capture students' existing mental model before instruction.

---

# 10–22 min — Independent Input: Khan Academy

## Student action

Students independently watch the Khan Academy video linked inside the workbook.

They complete the six-question **Video Note-Catcher** directly in Markdown:

1. What is a program?
2. What does a programmer do?
3. How does the video distinguish programming from computer science?
4. What questions might computer scientists study?
5. What fields/topics appear under the CS umbrella?
6. What idea changed, confirmed, or challenged the student's first definition?

Then students write:

> **Definition — Version 2**

and record:

- one claim they feel confident about;
- one claim for which they still need evidence.

## Teacher role

Students should control playback themselves when possible: pause, replay, and take notes at their own pace.

Do not turn the video into a teacher-led lecture.

---

# 22–35 min — Evidence Investigation

## Student action

Students use **Section 3 — Evidence Investigation** in their own Markdown file.

The workbook already contains:

- the 9-field CS Field Bank;
- Evidence Items **A–R**;
- the Evidence Hunt response prompts.

Students read all 18 evidence items and identify:

- two that clearly involve programming;
- two that are clearly CS but not simply writing code;
- one that spans multiple fields;
- one that expands or complicates the Khan Academy model.

Students then return to the uncertain claim from Version 2 and use a specific evidence letter to test it.

## Teacher role

Circulate and ask students for evidence rather than answers:

- “Which evidence item supports that?”
- “Could this belong to another field?”
- “Is the activity programming, or is programming being used to investigate something larger?”

Do not reveal the Teacher Key yet.

---

# 35–48 min — Pair Discussion

## Student action

Students compare their Markdown files with one partner.

Each student records in **their own file**:

- one disagreement;
- both positions;
- the evidence used;
- a pair working definition;
- an explanation of where programming fits into CS;
- one remaining uncertainty.

Students may revise their thinking, but **must not delete Versions 1 or 2**.

## Teacher role

Listen for productive disagreements, especially:

- CS = coding;
- anything involving a computer = CS;
- interface design is “not technical”;
- software engineering is identical to programming;
- AI is separate from CS.

Use these observations to decide what clarification is actually needed later.

---

# 48–60 min — Team CS Field Map + Definition Version 3

## Student action

Teams of 3–4 complete the **digital CS Field Map** in the workbook.

Instead of moving paper cards, students place Evidence Bank letters into the Markdown table:

| CS Field | Evidence Letter(s) | Justification |
|---|---|---|

A letter may appear in multiple fields if the team can justify the overlap.

Each student records the team's decisions in their own file.

Teams must also identify:

- one cross-field evidence item;
- one item beginners might incorrectly call “just coding”;
- one disputed placement.

Then each student writes:

> **Definition — Version 3**

and explains what changed across:

> **Version 1 → Version 2 → Version 3**

## Teacher clarification — only now

After students have produced evidence, provide only the clarification needed.

A useful working definition is:

> **Computer science studies computation and information: what can be computed, how information is represented and processed, how algorithms and computational systems work, how efficiently and reliably they work, and how computing systems interact with people and society.**

Clarify as needed:

- **programming** = expressing computational procedures and building executable systems;
- **software engineering** = disciplined development and maintenance of software systems;
- **computer engineering** = stronger emphasis on hardware/electrical computing systems;
- **IT** = operation, deployment, administration, and support of computing infrastructure.

Do not require students to copy the teacher definition.

---

# 60–78 min — AP CSP Discovery

## Student action

Students continue in **Section 6 — AP CSP Discovery** of the same Markdown file.

The workbook already includes:

- simplified AP CSP Big Ideas;
- simplified Computational Thinking Practices;
- the official College Board reference link;
- Course Experience Bank **S1–S8**;
- an AP CSP mapping table;
- an AP CSP gap checklist.

Students map these real course experiences:

- user interviews;
- Figma prototyping;
- GitHub workflow;
- JavaScript algorithms;
- debugging;
- data processing;
- usability testing;
- AI bias and limitations.

For each experience they choose the strongest AP CSP connection and explain why.

`No Strong Direct Match` is allowed.

Students then identify:

- two AP CSP areas this course genuinely supports;
- two AP CSP areas that still require separate AP CSP instruction;
- one experience that connects to multiple AP CSP ideas/practices.

## Teacher role

Do not force mappings to make the course appear more AP-aligned than it really is.

The intended conclusion is:

> This course develops authentic computing, software-development, design, and responsible-computing practices that support parts of AP CSP, but it is not a substitute for AP CSP's complete content and assessment preparation.

---

# 78–87 min — Individual Transfer

## Student action

Students complete the **Exit Check** and final transfer prompt in their own Markdown file.

Transfer prompt:

> A student says, “This class is basically a web-coding class.”
>
> Use at least two specific pieces of evidence from today's video, Evidence Bank, field map, or AP CSP discovery to disagree, qualify, or agree.

The response must cite specific evidence rather than general opinion.

# Success Evidence

Students can:

- distinguish computer science from programming without treating them as unrelated;
- explain why programming is important but not the whole of CS;
- identify multiple CS fields using concrete evidence;
- recognize that CS fields overlap;
- revise a conceptual model after receiving new input and evidence;
- identify genuine AP CSP overlaps without forcing alignment;
- identify important AP CSP content this course does not replace;
- preserve their learning process as versioned Markdown evidence.

# Assessment Stance

Do not grade students on memorizing a perfect definition of computer science or AP CSP terminology.

Assess:

- use of evidence;
- quality of reasoning;
- visible revision across Versions 1–3;
- defensible classification;
- transfer to the “web-coding class” claim.

The central learning outcome is not a sentence to memorize. It is a more accurate mental model of **what computer science is and where this course sits inside it**.
