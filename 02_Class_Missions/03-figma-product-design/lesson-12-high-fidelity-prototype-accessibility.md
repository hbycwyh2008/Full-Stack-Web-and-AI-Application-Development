# Lesson 12 — High-Fidelity Prototype and Accessibility

## Mission
Turn the validated structure into a visually coherent, accessible, interactive prototype.

## Core Artifact
Clickable high-fidelity Figma prototype.

## Launch Challenge — 0–10
Compare interface examples for contrast, readable type, target size, focus/feedback, and hierarchy. Mark barriers before discussing aesthetics.

## Evidence / Resource Scan — 10–25
Use an accessibility checklist and the team's research/requirements as constraints. Students identify which design decisions affect real users most.

## Team Sensemaking — 25–40
Teams define a small visual system: typography, spacing, color roles, component states, and responsive behavior. Every decorative choice must not weaken usability.

## Studio Build — 40–65
Create high-fidelity screens for the primary flow, including empty/error/success states where relevant. Connect them into a clickable prototype.

## Critique / Test — 65–80
Peer reviewers use a task and accessibility checklist. They do not give “I like it” feedback; they report observed friction and criterion failures.

## Ship Evidence — 80–90
Revise at least two accessibility/usability issues and record before/after screenshots or notes.

## Success Evidence
The prototype supports the task, communicates system status, and has explicit accessibility decisions.
