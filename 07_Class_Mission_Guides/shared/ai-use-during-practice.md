# AI Use During Class Practice

AI tools (ChatGPT, Cursor, etc.) can **help you learn** in this course. They **cannot** replace your thinking or your evidence.

![AI Use Policy — Use AI as a learning tool, not a shortcut](../../05_Public_Documents/posters/ai-use-policy.png)

*Course poster: [ai-use-policy.png](../../05_Public_Documents/posters/ai-use-policy.png)*

---

## You MAY Use AI For

- **Hints** — “What does this error mean?”
- **Explanations** — “Explain `git commit` in simple words”
- **Debugging suggestions** — after you read the error yourself
- **Checking** your understanding — “Is my commit message clear?”

Always **verify** AI answers. AI can be wrong.

---

## You MAY NOT Use AI For

- Generating the **full answer** during **Independent Rebuild** (60–72 min)
- Finishing the mission without understanding
- Copy-pasting README or reflections you cannot explain
- Bypassing debugging — “fix my repo” with no attempt first

If your teacher says “no AI this block,” follow that rule.

---

## If AI Helped You

You must still:

1. **Read** what AI produced
2. **Change** it so it is accurate and in your words
3. **Test** your work (run, commit, check GitHub)
4. **Explain** it orally or in your learning log

---

## Oral Explanation

You may be **randomly asked** to explain your work — especially if AI was used.

Be ready to say:

- what you built or committed
- what you changed after AI
- what you still do not understand

---

## Disclose Your AI Use

When AI helped you, include in your README, learning log, or project reflection:

```text
AI Help Used:
What I asked AI:
How it helped me:
What I did on my own:
```
