# 06 — AI Literacy: Thinking Clearly About Artificial Intelligence

**Primary text:** Melanie Mitchell, _Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans_.

Official book page: https://melaniemitchell.me/aibook/

This is a **four-lesson concentrated unit**. It comes after students have learned the main software-development and JavaScript foundations and before final testing/capstone work.

The purpose is AI literacy itself. Students do **not** need to force every reading into a website/product-design implication.

## Lessons

- [Lesson 21 — What Is AI? History, Learning, and Claims](lesson-21-what-is-ai.md)
- [Lesson 22 — Vision, Learning, Generalization, and Failure](lesson-22-learning-generalization-failure.md)
- [Lesson 23 — Games, Language, and Apparent Intelligence](lesson-23-games-language-intelligence.md)
- [Lesson 24 — Understanding, Abstraction, Analogy, and the Limits of AI](lesson-24-understanding-abstraction-analogy.md)

## Reading Approach

Page numbers vary by edition. Assign selected chapters/sections rather than copying book text into this repository.

Suggested grouping:

- Lesson 21: Chapters 1–3, selected sections;
- Lesson 22: Chapters 4–7, selected sections;
- Lesson 23: Chapters 8–13, selected sections;
- Lesson 24: Chapters 14–16, selected sections.

Students do not need to summarize every chapter. Class time is for comparing claims with evidence, analyzing failures, and constructing arguments.

## Core AI Literacy Standard

Students should become able to distinguish:

- performance from understanding;
- training success from generalization;
- narrow competence from general intelligence;
- fluent language from grounded meaning;
- evidence from anthropomorphic interpretation;
- technical capability from social/ethical consequences.
