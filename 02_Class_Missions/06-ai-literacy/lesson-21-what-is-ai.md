# Lesson 21 — Definitions, Methods, and Learning

**Students:** Grade 7  
**Delivery:** three 45-minute class meetings: **21A, 21B, and 21C**  
**Only required content source:** Melanie Mitchell, *Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans*  
**Student preparation:** none  
**Regular homework:** none  
**Flow:** [Grade 7 AI Literacy 45-Minute Classroom Flow](classroom-flow-45-min.md)  
**Reading locations:** [Required In-Class Reading Map](required-reading-map.md)

## Lesson Purpose

Students begin with the book itself. They examine why “intelligence” is hard to define, compare two broad families of AI methods, and separate evidence about neural-network learning from larger expectations about general intelligence.

## Required Mastery

By the end of Meeting 21C, students can:

1. separate observed behavior from an interpretation about understanding or consciousness;
2. explain that AI is a field with more than one approach;
3. distinguish explicit symbolic rules from learned subsymbolic patterns at an introductory level;
4. explain the basic role of weights and error correction in the book's digit-recognition example;
5. separate a demonstrated technical result from a prediction about broad or general intelligence.

## Teacher Setup for All Three Meetings

- Locate the exact passages listed in the [Required In-Class Reading Map](required-reading-map.md) and write the page ranges into the teacher copy before class.
- Keep each meeting's total within its stated **300–450-word** range.
- Display only the four required vocabulary words listed for that meeting.
- Prepare the small organizer named in the Mission Task; one half-page is enough.
- Do not assign advance reading or add a video, article, chatbot demonstration, or second AI text.

## Meeting 21A — What Counts as Intelligence?

**Essential question:** What evidence is needed before we call a machine intelligent?

**Required in-class reading — 300–400 words:** two short chunks: (1) the Prologue passage about Douglas Hofstadter's changed reaction to recent AI progress; (2) the Chapter 1 passage on intelligence as a many-meaning concept and AI's scientific and practical goals. Use the exact start/stop anchors and exclusions in [21A of the reading map](required-reading-map.md#21a--what-counts-as-intelligence).

| Time | Classroom-flow block | Required student action |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–3 | **Entry Point Check** | Choose which claim needs stronger evidence: “The machine gives a correct answer” or “The machine understands.” Write one reason. |
| 3–17 | **Individual Learning** | Read both assigned chunks in class. Mark C, E, and ?. After each chunk, write a margin paraphrase of no more than ten words. Finish the ready line: “Mitchell's point is ___ because ___.” |
| 17–22 | **Talk Round 1** | Partners use: “The text shows ___ on page ___; a stronger interpretation would be ___.” Agree on one claim boundary. |
| 22–28 | **Entry Points Check / Teacher Diagnosis** | Pairs show their claim and evidence. Teacher diagnoses one shared confusion about behavior, intelligence, understanding, consciousness, or AI's two goals and briefly rereads the relevant book sentence. |
| 28–38 | **Guided Practice / Mission Task** | Complete a **Claim–Interpretation Boundary Card**. Teacher models the first box; students add: observed behavior, possible interpretation, exact book evidence, and what is not yet proved. |
| 38–43 | **Exit Check** | Independently complete the four lines for Meeting 21A in the [Learning Log](learning-log-template.md). The book case and page number are required. |
| 43–45 | **Submission** | Submit the Boundary Card and Learning Log entry. |

### Claim–Interpretation Boundary Card

| What was observed? | What might someone conclude? | What book evidence supports that conclusion? | What is not proved? |
|---|---|---|---|
| one short phrase | one short phrase | case/detail + page | one short phrase |

**Success evidence:** The student does not treat successful behavior as automatic proof of an inner state.

## Meeting 21B — Is AI One Method?

**Essential question:** Is artificial intelligence one kind of technology or a field with different approaches?

**Required in-class reading — 300–400 words:** one Chapter 1 selection describing the field's competing methods, then contrasting symbolic and subsymbolic approaches and locating machine learning/deep learning within that contrast. Use the exact start/stop anchor and exclusions in [21B of the reading map](required-reading-map.md#21b--is-ai-one-method).

| Time | Classroom-flow block | Required student action |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–3 | **Entry Point Check** | Respond to one either/or prompt: “Must an AI always follow rules written one by one by a person?” Circle yes, no, or unsure and give one reason. |
| 3–17 | **Individual Learning** | Read the assigned Chapter 1 selection. Pause at the symbolic/subsymbolic transition. Mark one C and one E for each approach, then write one eight-to-ten-word paraphrase per approach. |
| 17–22 | **Talk Round 1** | Partner A explains symbolic; Partner B explains subsymbolic. Switch and correct one another using: “I think ___ because the text says/describes ___ on page ___.” |
| 22–28 | **Entry Points Check / Teacher Diagnosis** | Pairs supply one accurate similarity and difference. Teacher clarifies only a demonstrated confusion among symbolic, subsymbolic, machine learning, and deep learning. |
| 28–38 | **Guided Practice / Mission Task** | Complete a two-row **Methods Comparison**. Teacher models the representation column; students fill how behavior is produced, one book-based strength/use, and one claim the passage does not justify. |
| 38–43 | **Exit Check** | Independently complete the four Learning Log lines for Meeting 21B. Use one comparison detail and a page number. |
| 43–45 | **Submission** | Submit the Methods Comparison and Learning Log entry. |

### Methods Comparison

| Approach | What represents knowledge? | How is behavior produced? | What can this approach do? | What does this passage not prove? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Symbolic | short phrase | short phrase | one book-based point | one boundary |
| Subsymbolic | short phrase | short phrase | one book-based point | one boundary |

**Success evidence:** The student describes AI as a field of approaches and makes a basic, text-supported distinction between symbolic rules and learned numerical patterns.

## Meeting 21C — Learning Evidence and AI Expectations

**Essential question:** What did learning systems demonstrate, and what did people predict from that evidence?

**Required in-class reading — 350–450 words:** two chunks: (1) Chapter 2's digit-recognition explanation, from inputs/learned weights through the basic purpose of backpropagation; (2) Chapter 3's AI-spring passage contrasting narrow/task-specific AI with general intelligence. Use the exact anchors and exclusions in [21C of the reading map](required-reading-map.md#21c--learning-evidence-and-ai-expectations).

| Time | Classroom-flow block | Required student action |
|---:|---|---|
| 0–3 | **Entry Point Check** | Put these in a possible learning order: make a guess, compare with the correct answer, adjust, try again. Add one question. |
| 3–17 | **Individual Learning** | Read the two assigned chunks. For Chunk 1, mark input, weight, error, and adjustment. For Chunk 2, mark one demonstrated success and one broader expectation. Write one short paraphrase after each chunk. |
| 17–22 | **Talk Round 1** | Partners first reconstruct the learning loop without equations, then use: “The result supports ___; it does not yet support ___.” Cite one page from each chapter. |
| 22–28 | **Entry Points Check / Teacher Diagnosis** | Pairs show one four-step learning loop and one evidence boundary. Teacher clarifies only shared confusion about weights, backpropagation, narrow AI, or general AI. |
| 28–38 | **Guided Practice / Mission Task** | Build an **Evidence–Expectation Chain** with four boxes: mechanism, demonstrated result, larger prediction, and missing evidence. Teacher fills only the mechanism example; students complete the other boxes from the passages. |
| 38–43 | **Exit Check** | Independently complete the four Learning Log lines for Meeting 21C. The boundary line must contrast narrow success with a general-intelligence claim. |
| 43–45 | **Submission** | Submit the Evidence–Expectation Chain and Learning Log entry. |

### Evidence–Expectation Chain

| How learning changes the system | Demonstrated result | Larger expectation someone might form | Evidence still needed |
|---|---|---|---|
| one short mechanism | exact book result | one prediction | one missing test |

**Success evidence:** The student explains a simple error-adjustment loop and keeps the reported result separate from a forecast about general intelligence.

## Finish-Up Rule

All required reading and mission work happen during class. If time is lost, only unfinished Learning Log lines may be completed later, normally within five minutes.
