# HCI Needfinding + Brainstorming — Unit Test Teacher Key

**Test:** [HCI_User_Research_Unit_Test.md](HCI_User_Research_Unit_Test.md)  
**Total:** 35 points

Accept equivalent wording when the reasoning is correct.

---

## Part A — Diagnose the Research Plan (8 pts)

### 1. — 2 pts

Expected:

- starting claim assumes an **AI planner** before needfinding;
- “students always forget” is unsupported/overgeneralized.

Strong alternatives:

> “How do students currently keep track of homework, and where does that process break down?”

or

> “What causes students to lose track of assignments?”

### 2. — 3 pts

1 pt each for three distinct valid issues with explanation:

- narrow/convenience sample;
- leading wording;
- hypothetical intention instead of behavior;
- coaching/facilitation effect during observation;
- overgeneralization;
- confirmation bias.

### 3. — 3 pts

1 pt each for a defensible improvement:

- neutral recent-behavior interview question;
- goal-based observation without coaching;
- broader/more relevant sample.

4. **Q** — 1 pt  
5. **A** — 1 pt  
6. **I** — 1 pt  
7. **P** — 1 pt

### 8. — 2 pts

Statement 5 is directly observable. Statement 6 infers an internal state and a cause that were not directly observed.

---

## Part C — Survey Question Quality (10 pts)

### 9. — 2 pts

Problem: double-barreled / **Clear**.

Strong response splits reminders and calendar notifications into separate questions.

### 10. — 2 pts

Problem: leading / **Unbiased**.

Remove “amazing” and do not assume usefulness.

### 11. — 2 pts

Problem: vague / **Specific**.

Add a concrete behavior and time frame.

Example:

> “During the past 7 days, on how many days did you study outside scheduled class time?”

### 12. — 2 pts

Problem: overlapping ranges.

Example:

- 0
- 1–2
- 3–4
- 5–6
- 7+

### 13. — 2 pts

Expected idea: future intention may not match actual behavior; recent behavior gives stronger needfinding evidence.

Accept any concrete recent/current-behavior replacement.

---

## Part D — Sample and Claims (3 pts)

### 14.

**Correct: C**

- 1 pt: C
- 2 pts: explanation recognizes sample limits and/or lack of causal/feature evidence.

---

## Part E — Evidence → Need (3 pts)

### 15.

Strong example:

> **When students need to find an assignment, they need to identify where it is located without repeatedly searching across multiple platforms so that they can begin the task efficiently.**

- 2 pts: need is evidence-backed and feature-neutral.
- 1 pt: limitation correctly constrains the claim, e.g. Grade 7 / one school only.

---

## Part F — Brainstorming / Design Alternatives (5 pts)

### 16. — 2 pts

Award full credit when four directions are meaningfully different in interaction structure.

Possible examples:

- unified assignment dashboard;
- calendar/timeline;
- search-first interface;
- subject-card/browser approach;
- notification-centered flow;
- conversational assistant.

Do not award full credit for color/font/layout-only variants.

### 17. — 1 pt

Expected:

> These are not genuinely different alternatives; they are the same interaction structure with different visual styling. The team is showing design fixation / insufficient divergence.

### 18. — 2 pts

- 1 pt: choice is connected to the evidence-backed need/task.
- 1 pt: names a real uncertainty worth prototyping/testing.
