# 06 — AI Literacy: Reading *Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans*

**Core text and sole content input:** Melanie Mitchell, *Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans*  
**Delivery:** four thematic lessons, taught as **eight 45-minute class meetings**  
**Student pre-class work:** none  
**Regular homework:** none

Official book page: https://melaniemitchell.me/aibook/

## Unit Purpose

Students learn AI literacy by reading Mitchell's own arguments in class, reconstructing the evidence she uses, testing the boundaries of her claims, and revising their own judgements.

This is not a general AI survey assembled from videos, articles, product demos, or chatbot outputs. The book is the intellectual spine and the only required content source.

The unit is also not required to force every reading into a website, product-design, or machine-learning implication. Its purpose is careful reading and careful judgement about artificial intelligence.

## Non-Negotiable Delivery Rules

1. All required reading begins and ends during class.
2. Each meeting uses one teacher-selected passage from the book, normally **2–4 pages** or an amount students can read closely in about ten minutes.
3. Students are not expected to read complete chapters outside class.
4. A vocabulary gloss, question sheet, or graphic organizer may support access, but it may not introduce a second content source.
5. Videos, news articles, current product pages, live chatbot demonstrations, and unrelated AI courses are not part of the required input.
6. Cases used for analysis come from the assigned Mitchell selection. Teacher-created hypothetical prompts may test transfer, but they may not replace the book evidence.
7. The Learning Log is the Exit Check. It should normally be completed before students leave.
8. If the Learning Log is unfinished, the only finish-up work is the remaining entry, capped at approximately five minutes. There is no additional homework.

## Why Four Lesson Files but Eight Meetings?

The original unit contained four 90-minute lessons. Each lesson is now divided into **Meeting A** and **Meeting B**, preserving the original 360 minutes of class time without renumbering Lessons 25–32 in later course phases.

## Reading Selection Rule

Because page numbers differ by edition, every lesson plan identifies chapters and selection criteria rather than copying the book.

Before each meeting, the teacher records:

- edition;
- chapter and section title;
- exact page range;
- three to five access vocabulary terms;
- the one claim students must reconstruct from the selection.

The selected passage must contain enough of Mitchell's reasoning for students to identify:

```text
claim → book evidence or case → supported conclusion → claim boundary
```

A passage that contains only background description is insufficient for the mission.

## Learning Log

Students complete one concise entry during each meeting. The log is not a chapter summary. It records:

1. Mitchell's claim;
2. evidence or a case from the assigned pages;
3. what that evidence does not prove;
4. the student's revised judgement or remaining question.

Use [AI Literacy Learning Log](learning-log-template.md).

## End-of-Unit Standard

By the end of Meeting 24B, students can independently:

- distinguish performance from understanding;
- distinguish benchmark or task success from broad generalization;
- distinguish narrow competence from general intelligence;
- distinguish fluent language from grounded meaning;
- connect a claim to evidence from the book;
- state the boundary of that evidence;
- revise a judgement when the evidence changes.
