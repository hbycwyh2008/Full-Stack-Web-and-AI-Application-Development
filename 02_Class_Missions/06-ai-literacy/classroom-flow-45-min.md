# AI Literacy — Canonical 45-Minute Classroom Flow

This is the 45-minute adaptation of the course's required [Classroom Flow](../shared/classroom-flow.md). The **names, order, and purpose of the seven blocks remain unchanged**. Only the timing is compressed.

## Seven Required Blocks

| Time | Block | Student role | Teacher role |
|---:|---|---|---|
| **0–4 min** | **Entry Point Check** | Make an individual prediction, classification, or judgement before reading | Expose prior ideas; do not front-load Mitchell's answer |
| **4–14 min** | **Individual Learning** | Read the assigned Mitchell passage and mark a claim, evidence, and uncertainty | Provide the exact book location and access vocabulary; do not add a second content source |
| **14–19 min** | **Talk Round 1** | Compare evidence, explain reasoning, disagree, and revise with a partner | Listen for shared misunderstandings and select what needs diagnosis |
| **19–25 min** | **Entry Points Check / Teacher Diagnosis** | Produce one group claim and one unresolved point | Clarify only a demonstrated reading barrier; keep direct explanation brief |
| **25–37 min** | **Guided Practice / Mission Task** | Apply the book's reasoning to a book case, structured comparison, or transfer prompt | Coach students to connect claims to specific book evidence and name boundaries |
| **37–43 min** | **Exit Check** | Independently complete the meeting's Learning Log response or final transfer | Check whether each student can reconstruct the reasoning without group language |
| **43–45 min** | **Submission** | Submit the named artifact and Learning Log entry | Verify that the evidence is present and attributable to the assigned reading |

Total: **45 minutes**.

## One Required Resource Rule

The sole content input is Melanie Mitchell's *Artificial Intelligence: A Guide for Thinking Humans*.

Allowed access supports:

- a three-to-five-term vocabulary gloss;
- a blank claim–evidence organizer;
- teacher directions and timing prompts;
- a teacher-created hypothetical used only after the book reading to test transfer.

Not used as required input:

- videos or podcasts;
- news or product articles;
- live AI/chatbot demonstrations;
- online chapter summaries;
- a second AI textbook or course;
- AI-generated summaries in place of reading.

## Reading Volume

Select the amount students can read closely in ten minutes. For most classes, this is approximately 2–4 pages, but text difficulty and English reading support take priority over page count.

Do not solve a timing problem by asking students to finish the chapter at home.

## Teacher Diagnosis Limit

Teacher Diagnosis is not a scheduled lecture. Use this sequence:

1. display one student/group claim;
2. identify the exact reading misunderstanding;
3. clarify it in no more than a few minutes;
4. send students immediately into the Mission Task.

## Evidence and Finish-Up Rule

Each meeting ends with:

- one named in-class reasoning artifact; and
- one concise Learning Log entry, which serves as the Exit Check.

There is no separate routine homework. When time is lost, only the unfinished Learning Log or exit response may be completed later, normally within five minutes.
