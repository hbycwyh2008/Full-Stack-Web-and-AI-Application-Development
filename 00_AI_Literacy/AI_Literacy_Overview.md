# AI Literacy Overview

## Why AI Literacy Comes Early (Phase 1)

You start with **Git & GitHub (Phase 0)** and **Notion (Phase 1)**. **Phase 2** builds a **unified** AI literacy foundation before the **AI Math Bridge (Phase 3)** and coding phases.

Before you build the **AI School Assistant**, you need to understand what AI is, what it can do, and what it cannot do. AI literacy is not a separate theory class — it connects directly to the final project.

Store your Phase 1 work in:

```text
student-portfolio/ai-literacy/
```

---

## What You Will Study

This phase uses two DeepLearning.AI courses by Andrew Ng:

| Course | Focus |
|---|---|
| **AI for Everyone** | What AI is, what it can and cannot do, how it affects society |
| **Generative AI for Everyone** | How generative AI works, prompt engineering, responsible use |

Together, these courses give you the vocabulary and judgment you need before you write code or connect an LLM API.

---

## How This Connects to the AI School Assistant

The final project is a web app that answers questions from school documents. AI literacy helps you answer questions like:

```text
Why should the assistant say "I don't know" instead of guessing?
Why do we need source documents before generating an answer?
What is the difference between a chatbot and a search engine?
How should students use AI responsibly when building this app?
```

Every concept in this phase should connect back to building a **useful, honest, and responsible** AI School Assistant.

---

## Phase Goals

By the end of this phase, you should be able to:

- Explain what AI and machine learning mean in plain language.
- Describe what generative AI can and cannot do.
- Use basic prompt engineering ideas to get better AI responses.
- Identify risks such as hallucinations, bias, and over-reliance on AI.
- Apply an "AI lens" when planning and evaluating the AI School Assistant.
