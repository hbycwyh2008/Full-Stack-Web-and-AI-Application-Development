# Generative AI for Everyone — Study Guide

**Course:** Generative AI for Everyone  
**Instructor:** Andrew Ng  
**Provider:** DeepLearning.AI  
**Link:** https://www.deeplearning.ai/courses/generative-ai-for-everyone

Complete this guide **after** AI for Everyone. This course goes deeper into generative AI — the type of AI your final project will use.

---

## Before You Start

- Review your notes from AI for Everyone.
- Keep your **AI Literacy Notes** page open.
- Try the hands-on prompt exercises when the course asks you to.

---

## Module 1: Introduction to Generative AI

### Key Ideas to Capture
- What is generative AI?
- How is it different from traditional AI that only classifies or predicts?
- What is an LLM (large language model)?
- What does "generating text" actually mean?

### Connect to AI School Assistant
> Your app will use an LLM to generate answers. What does the model **generate**, and what does your **code** still need to control?

---

## Module 2: How Generative AI Works (High Level)

### Key Ideas to Capture
- What is training data?
- What is a prompt?
- What is a response / completion?
- Why do models sometimes make things up (hallucinations)?

### Connect to AI School Assistant
> Why must your assistant use **school documents** instead of trusting the model's memory alone?

---

## Module 3: Prompt Engineering Basics

### Key Ideas to Capture
- What makes a clear, useful prompt?
- How can you ask the model to stay on topic?
- How can you ask for step-by-step or structured answers?
- When should you rewrite a prompt instead of accepting a bad answer?

### Connect to AI School Assistant
> Write one sample prompt your backend might send to the LLM. Include: the user's question, the document text, and an instruction to cite the source.

---

## Module 4: Generative AI in Practice

### Key Ideas to Capture
- What are common workplace (and school) use cases?
- What is the difference between using AI as a helper vs. replacing your thinking?
- What does a good human + AI workflow look like?

### Connect to AI School Assistant
> When a student uses your app, where does the AI help, and where does the **student** still need to think for themselves?

---

## Module 5: Risks, Limits, and Responsible Use

### Key Ideas to Capture
- What are hallucinations, bias, and privacy risks?
- Why should users know when they are talking to AI?
- What does responsible disclosure mean when you build with AI?

### Connect to AI School Assistant
> What should your app show when it is **not confident** in an answer? Write the exact message a user should see.

---

## Module 6: Beyond Prompting (Preview for Later Phases)

### Key Ideas to Capture
- What is retrieval (finding relevant documents before answering)?
- Why is "retrieve then generate" useful?
- How does this idea connect to RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)?

### Connect to AI School Assistant
> Draw a simple flow: **Question → Find document → Send to LLM → Show answer + source**. You will build this in later phases.
