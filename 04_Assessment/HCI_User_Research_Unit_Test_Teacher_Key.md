# HCI User Research — Unit Test Teacher Key

**Test:** [HCI_User_Research_Unit_Test.md](HCI_User_Research_Unit_Test.md)  
**Total:** 30 points

This assessment is intentionally **application-heavy**. Accept equivalent wording when the reasoning is correct.

---

# Answer Key

## Part A — Diagnose the Research Plan (8 pts)

### 1. Need or feature? — 2 pts

Expected ideas:

- The claim assumes the solution is an **AI homework planner** before needfinding.
- “Students always forget” is an unsupported generalization.

Strong researchable alternatives include:

> “What causes students to miss or lose track of homework tasks?”

or

> “How do students currently track homework, and where does that process break down?”

**Scoring**
- 1 pt: identifies solution/feature assumption or unsupported claim.
- 1 pt: writes a neutral, researchable problem/unknown.

---

### 2. Bias check — 3 pts

Award 1 pt each for three distinct valid problems with a reasonable explanation.

Possible answers:

- **Convenience / narrow sample:** only close friends; may not represent other students.
- **Leading question:** wording assumes the AI planner will help.
- **Hypothetical-intention problem:** “Would you use…” predicts future behavior rather than measuring current behavior.
- **Observer / facilitation effect:** explaining every click can change how the participant behaves.
- **Overgeneralization:** evidence from three friends cannot support a claim about all middle-school students.
- **Confirmation bias:** plan is structured mainly to confirm the team's preselected solution.

---

### 3. Improve the plan — 3 pts

1 pt each for a defensible improvement.

Examples:

- **Interview:** ask neutral questions about recent real behavior, e.g. “Tell me about the last time you almost missed or missed an assignment.”
- **Observation:** give a task goal and observe without coaching; record actions separately from interpretations.
- **Sample:** include students beyond the team's friends, ideally different classes/organizational habits/grades as appropriate to the research question.

---

# Part B — Evidence or Interpretation? (6 pts)

### 4. Q — Quote — 1 pt

### 5. A — Action — 1 pt

### 6. I — Interpretation — 1 pt

### 7. P — Pattern — 1 pt

### 8. — 2 pts

Expected reasoning:

Statement 5 reports something directly observable. Statement 6 infers the participant's internal state (“confused”) and assigns a cause (“badly designed”) that was not directly observed or independently established.

- 1 pt: distinguishes observation from inference.
- 1 pt: explains why the causal/internal-state claim requires additional evidence.

# Part C — Survey Question Quality (10 pts)

## 9. — 2 pts

Original:

> “Do you like homework reminders and calendar notifications?”

Primary problem: **Clear** — double-barreled; asks about two things.

Strong rewrites split it:

> “How useful are homework reminders to you?”

and, separately,

> “How useful are calendar notifications to you?”

Accept other neutral, answerable rewrites.

- 1 pt: identifies double-barreled / clarity problem.
- 1 pt: separates the two constructs.

---

## 10. — 2 pts

Original:

> “How useful is our amazing AI study planner?”

Primary problem: **Unbiased** — “amazing” pushes a positive judgment and assumes usefulness.

Example rewrite:

> “After using the study planner, how useful or not useful was it for organizing your tasks?”

A balanced response scale may also be supplied.

- 1 pt: identifies leading/biased wording.
- 1 pt: neutral rewrite.

---

## 11. — 2 pts

Original:

> “Do you study often?”

Primary problem: **Specific** — “often” is ambiguous; no time frame or measurable behavior.

Example rewrite:

> “During the past 7 days, on how many days did you study outside scheduled class time?”

- 1 pt: identifies ambiguity/lack of specificity.
- 1 pt: adds a clear behavior and time frame.

---

## 12. — 2 pts

Problem: response ranges **overlap** at 2, 4, and 6, reducing usability/clarity.

Example:

- 0 times
- 1–2 times
- 3–4 times
- 5–6 times
- 7 or more times

- 1 pt: identifies overlapping ranges.
- 1 pt: provides non-overlapping, reasonably complete choices.

---

## 13. — 2 pts

Expected reasoning:

Hypothetical intention may not predict actual behavior. Needfinding is stronger when it examines what users recently did, experienced, or struggled with.

Possible replacement:

> “During the past 7 days, how did you keep track of homework from different classes?”

or

> “During the past 7 days, how many times did you have to check more than one place to find an assignment?”

- 1 pt: explains behavior > hypothetical intention.
- 1 pt: writes a concrete past/current behavior question.

---

# Part D — Sample and Claims (3 pts)

## 14.

**Correct answer: C**

> In this robotics-club sample, 14 of 18 respondents reported missing at least one deadline; other students may behave differently.

**Scoring**
- 1 pt: C.
- 2 pts: explanation recognizes at least two of the following:
  - the sample is restricted to robotics-club students;
  - 14/18 applies to respondents, not all school students;
  - the survey does not establish that students need a particular feature;
  - the survey does not establish LMS causation.

---

# Part E — Evidence → Need (3 pts)

## 15.

Example strong answer:

> **When students need to find an assignment, they can identify where the assignment is located without searching across multiple platforms so that they can begin the task efficiently.**

Accept equivalent **need-focused**, non-feature-specific statements supported by the evidence.

Valid limitation:

> The evidence comes only from Grade 7 students at one school, so it should not automatically be generalized to other grades or schools.

**Scoring**
- 2 pts: need is traceable to evidence, describes user task/outcome, and does not prescribe a feature.
- 1 pt: limitation is relevant and constrains the claim appropriately.

## Diagnostic strands

Do not use only the total score. Also inspect these strands:

| Strand | Items |
|---|---|
| Needfinding / research framing | 1, 3, 15 |
| Bias / method quality | 2, 3, 14 |
| Evidence vs interpretation | 4–8 |
| Survey-question quality | 9–13 |
| Sampling / claim strength | 2, 14, 15 |
| Evidence → need | 15 |

A student who scores well overall but cannot separate **evidence from interpretation** or repeatedly turns needs into features should revise before passing the Discover → Design gate.
